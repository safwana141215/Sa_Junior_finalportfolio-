Iterative Design Process

How I go from idea to finished game through planning, testing, and refinement.

1

Brainstorming & Concept

📝

Mind Map

I start by writing ideas and drawing mind maps. For Neon Runner, I analyzed games like Celeste to understand what makes platforming fun. I created a one-page concept document: "Fast platforming with time mechanics in a neon city."

2

Wireframes & Flowcharts

📐

Level Sketch

Before opening Unity, I draw level layouts on paper and create flowcharts showing game flow: Menu → Tutorial → Gameplay → Pause → Game Over. This planning prevents scope creep and keeps development focused.

3

Grey-Boxing & Prototyping

🧱

Grey-Box Screenshot

I build with simple cubes first—no art. For Neon Runner Level 3, grey-boxing took 2 hours and testing showed jumps were too hard. Adjusting platform distances took 5 minutes in the grey-box, saving hours of art rework. This proves: fun must come before aesthetics.

4

Playtesting & Refinement

🎮

Playtest Data

I run 3-5 structured playtest sessions per project, recording completion times and feedback. In Chrono Cube, players missed the rewind tutorial. The fix: a forced slow-motion sequence that shows the mechanic before asking players to use it. Tutorial dropout dropped from 40% to 5%.

5

Final Polish & GDD

✨

Final Build

I add art, sound, and UI polish while maintaining the core design. I document everything in a Game Design Document (GDD) including mechanics specs, level rationale, and post-mortem analysis. This shows professional workflow and helps me remember why I made each decision.
